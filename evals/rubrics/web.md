# Web app rubric

The reference is a *great companion site for an RPG*: the kind a player keeps open next to the game. It is judged on
what a player feels in the first ten seconds and after ten minutes, on a laptop and on a phone held in one hand.

Score every dimension 1-10 (5 = acceptable, 8 = good, 10 = nothing to add). Be strict: a 9 needs a reason.

| Dimension | What a 9-10 looks like |
| --- | --- |
| **hierarchy** | The eye lands on the one thing that matters on the page; secondary information is clearly secondary. |
| **clarity** | A new player understands every number, label and button without guessing; no jargon, no dead ends. |
| **density** | Enough information to decide something, never a wall; good use of the width; no big empty holes. |
| **consistency** | Same spacing, radius, colour meaning and wording for the same things on every page. |
| **mobile** | One-handed use works: thumb-sized targets, nothing cut off, nothing that needs zooming, sensible order. |
| **polish** | It feels finished: alignment, rhythm, states (empty, locked, done), no visual glitches. |
| **game-feel** | It makes progress and rarity feel rewarding: strong, readable status, a reason to come back. |
| **copy** | Italian that is short, natural and consistent in tone; no mixed languages, no clutter of emoji. |

For every problem you list give: severity (high / medium / low), where (page + viewport), what is wrong, and the
concrete change you would make (CSS/markup/wording level, not "improve spacing").
Judge only what you can see in the screenshots and the files you are given. Do not reward intent.
